Our story

Cooking for people we haven't met yet

The Long Table started at one kitchen table. It still feels like it.

The person behind it

Hi — I'm the cook.

The Long Table isn't a big operation with a warehouse and a fleet of vans. It's me, a well-loved kitchen, and a short list of growers I actually know by name. It grew out of cooking for friends' weddings and birthdays, and I've kept it small on purpose.

The rule hasn't changed since day one: cook food I'd be proud to put in front of my own family, treat every guest like one, and never let "catering" become a synonym for "fine, I guess." I take the food seriously so you don't have to take the day seriously — you just get to enjoy it.

Cook with me

How I work

Small on purpose

Because it's a one-person kitchen, the person who plans your menu is the same person who shops for it, cooks it, and turns up on the day. Nothing gets handed off to strangers, and nothing gets lost in translation.

For bigger events I bring on a couple of trusted hands to help serve — people I've cooked alongside for years — but the food always comes from one kitchen and one set of standards. It keeps things personal, and honestly, it keeps them good.

Sourcing & sustainability

Good food is a chain of good decisions

I can't fix everything, but I can be honest about where the food comes from and careful about what's left over.

Local & seasonal

I buy from a rotating handful of Willamette Valley farms and rebuild the menu each season, so ingredients are at their peak — and travel the shortest distance to your plate.

Low-waste service

Compostable or reusable service ware by default, careful portioning, and leftover donation partnerships so good food feeds people, not landfills.

Fair partners

I pay my growers and bakers fairly and never cut corners to hit a number. Food made under pressure tastes like it — so I don't work that way.

What I believe

A few things I won't budge on

Seasonal, always

If it isn't at its best right now, it isn't on the menu. That's the whole reason the menus change.

Everyone eats well

Vegan, gluten-free, allergies — never an afterthought. The whole table should get to enjoy the same meal.

No fuss, real food

Beautiful plates, honest flavours, and a host who actually gets to sit down and enjoy their own party.
